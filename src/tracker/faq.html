How is a swimmer's improvement rate calculated?

We draw a straight line through every race a swimmer has swum in one event at one pool length, and turn its slope into a percentage of their own average time per year. Their yearly rate is the middle value (the median) across all their events.

A line through every race, rather than first race against last, means one bad morning at either end cannot decide the result. "14.5%/yr faster" means their times are coming down by about 14.5% a year.

What is the benchmark under each rate?

Young swimmers improve far faster than older ones, whatever the coaching, so each swimmer is compared with the typical (median) rate of NextGen swimmers in their own age group over the same period.

"1.6%/yr more than 9-10 typically gain" means that swimmer is improving a little faster than most nine- and ten-year-olds at the club. If fewer than 6 swimmers in an age group are rated, the club-wide figure is used instead, and the card says so.

Why is my child in a lower group when they are getting faster?

The groups do not ask whether a swimmer is getting faster; nearly all of them are. They ask how fast, against what is typical for NextGen swimmers of the same age. Young swimmers improve quickly as a matter of course, so the bar for each age is different.

Swimmers are never ranked against each other. Each group is a fixed line, so no group has to be filled, and a swimmer only moves when their own rate, or what is typical for their age, changes.

What do the development groups mean?

Each swimmer's yearly rate is set against the typical rate for their age group:

  • Well ahead for their age: improving 6%/yr or more faster than is typical.
  • Ahead for their age: 2 to 6%/yr faster than is typical.
  • In line with their age: within 2%/yr of typical.
  • Below typical for their age: more than 2%/yr below typical, usually still getting faster.
  • Not enough data: not enough racing yet to measure a trend.

The groups are a guide to who to look at first, never a verdict on a child.

What do "vs form" and "pts" mean on a meet?

"vs form" compares every swim at that meet with the best that swimmer had already swum in the same event before the day, and takes the middle value. "−2%" means the team swam about 2% faster than their previous bests; "+1%" means about 1% slower.

It shows green at −2% or better, amber up to +0.6%, and red beyond that. "vs previous meet" counts how many percentage points (pts) that figure moved since the meet before: a minus number means the team swam closer to, or past, their bests than last time.

What does "rated" mean, and why are some swimmers not rated?

A swimmer is rated once there are enough races to measure a trend: at least 2 races in the same event and pool length, at least 60 days apart. Anything less would be a guess, not a measurement.

"Not enough data" is about the number of races, not about the swimmer. Each of those swimmers' cards says what is missing, which is usually a second meet.

What is the PB rate?

The share of a swimmer's races, or of a meet's swims, that were personal bests: the fastest that swimmer had ever swum in that event at that pool length. A swimmer's first-ever swim in an event counts as a personal best, which is why newer swimmers often have a high PB rate.

How do I read the progress chart?

A line that falls is a swimmer getting faster: times come down as they improve, so down the chart is progress. On a swimmer's own card, each line is one stroke at one distance and pool length, race by race.

In "Progress", each dot is one meet: how much faster the swimmer was than their first swim in each event, for one stroke. −5% means five per cent quicker. The grey line is the typical swimmer their age, measured the same way. 25m and 50m pools are never mixed on one line.

Tap a time on a swimmer's card to open the meet it was swum at.

Where does the data come from?

From the timing system's own result files, the same ones meet organisers publish, read field by field with nothing typed in by hand. Each meet is checked against the official results PDF before it is loaded.

Club figures count races from the day a swimmer joined NextGen; races swum earlier, or for another club, stay on that swimmer's own chart. If anything here contradicts your own record, your record is right, and we want to hear about it.
